Here's an explanation of the SARS-CoV-2 transcriptomics research that high school students can understand:
We studied how the SARS-CoV-2 virus, which causes COVID-19, affects the cells in our body. To do this, we looked at the genetic material inside the cells, called RNA, to see which genes were turned on or off when the virus was present. This is called transcriptomics. By analyzing the RNA, we can understand how the virus interacts with our cells and how our cells respond to the infection.

What we found was that when the SARS-CoV-2 virus infects a cell, it changes the way the cell behaves. The virus turns on some genes that help it replicate and spread, and turns off other genes that would normally help the cell fight off the infection. We also found that different types of cells in the body respond to the virus in different ways. For example, lung cells and immune cells had different patterns of gene expression when infected with the virus. This helps us understand why some people may be more susceptible to severe COVID-19 symptoms.

So, why does this matter? Understanding how the SARS-CoV-2 virus interacts with our cells can help us develop new treatments for COVID-19. By identifying the specific genes and pathways that are affected by the virus, we can design drugs that target those areas and help the body fight off the infection. This research can also help us understand why some people are more likely to get severely ill from COVID-19, and how we can protect them.

What's next? Now that we have a better understanding of how the SARS-CoV-2 virus affects our cells, we can use this information to develop new treatments and vaccines. We can also use this knowledge to study other viruses and how they interact with our cells, which can help us prepare for future pandemics. Additionally, this research can help us understand how to make our bodies more resilient to infections, which can lead to new discoveries in the field of medicine.
